# Predictions

Claims recorded **before** any return data was examined.

The point of this file is to make the project falsifiable by someone reading it
later. A prediction written after seeing results is not a prediction; it is a
description. Everything below was committed while the study had produced scores
but no returns, no event study, and no backtest — none of that code had been run
on real data.

Each entry states what was believed, why, what would confirm it, and what would
refute it. Outcomes get filled in afterwards, and are not edited to match.

---

## P1 — Evasion is where phrase-matching fails worst

**Recorded:** 1 September 2026
**Sample at the time:** 445 transcripts, 17 companies. Scores computed;
no price data downloaded, no returns analysed.

### The claim

Of the six rubric axes, **evasion** will show the largest improvement when the
LLM scorer replaces the lexical (regex) scorer — measured as the increase in
that axis's own predictive t-statistic on drift, and as a reduction in its share
of exactly-zero readings.

### Why

The lexical scorer measures evasion by matching about ten fixed phrases
("we don't break that out", "let's take that offline"). Across 345 usable calls
from 14 firms, that produced:

| axis | mean | std | exactly zero |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| hedging | 0.346 | 0.124 | 1% |
| forward ratio | 0.683 | 0.156 | 0% |
| externalisation | 0.834 | 0.168 | 0% |
| pushback | 0.394 | 0.246 | 16% |
| commitment | 0.063 | 0.064 | 27% |
| **evasion** | **0.032** | **0.047** | **62%** |

Evasion is zero on nearly two thirds of calls. That is not because executives
answer directly two thirds of the time — it is because a dodge can be phrased
in unlimited ways and a fixed list catches almost none of them. "We're
monitoring that closely", "I'd frame it differently", "that's not really how we
look at the business" are all evasions, and none are in the list.

The other five axes are either dense (hedging, forward ratio, externalisation
fire on essentially every call) or structural rather than lexical (pushback
counts analyst follow-up turns, which needs no vocabulary at all). Evasion is
the axis most dependent on guessing the right words in advance, so it is where
reading comprehension should beat pattern matching by the widest margin.

There is also a mechanical cost. When a firm's trailing four calls all score
zero, the baseline dispersion is zero and gets floored at 0.02. A single
non-zero reading then produces a large z-score generated by one lucky phrase
match. Sparsity does not merely lose signal here; it manufactures noise.

### What would confirm it

- Evasion's exactly-zero share falls substantially under LLM scoring.
- Evasion's standalone t-statistic on drift improves more than any other axis.

### What would refute it

- Another axis improves more.
- Evasion's zero share stays high under LLM scoring, implying calls genuinely
  contain little evasion rather than the measure missing it.
- No axis improves — the LLM adds nothing over regex, which is a legitimate and
  reportable outcome.

### Why this was not simply fixed

Broadening the evasion word list by reading these transcripts and adding the
dodges found in them would fit the instrument to the exact data it is about to
be tested on. The lexicons were broadened once already, when four of six axes
returned near-constant values on real text — that corrected a clear defect.
Tuning an axis that works but is sparse is a different thing, and it is the
researcher-degrees-of-freedom problem this project exists to avoid.

Leaving the weakness in place and predicting it in advance is worth more than
quietly patching it.

## P2 — The headline effect will not survive costs

**Recorded:** 1 September 2026

### The claim

The long/short strategy will not show a net Sharpe reliably above zero after
10bps of transaction costs. Specifically: the bootstrap 95% confidence interval
on the net Sharpe will include zero, and the deflated Sharpe ratio will fall
below 0.95.

### Why

Base rates. Most published anomalies fail out of sample, most language-based
signals correlate with the earnings surprise the market already priced, and the
announcement-window control in this design strips out precisely the component
most likely to carry the effect. A 24-firm large-cap universe over six years is
also thin — roughly 600 events after exclusions.

### What would confirm it

A net Sharpe whose confidence interval spans zero, or a deflated Sharpe below
0.95 once every specification tried is counted.

### What would refute it

A net Sharpe interval clearly above zero with a deflated Sharpe above 0.95, and
a coefficient that survives the announcement-window control.

### Why record a prediction of failure

So that a null result reads as a confirmed hypothesis rather than a
disappointment, and so that a positive result has to clear a bar that was set
before it appeared. If the effect does show up, this file is the evidence that
it was not the outcome being hunted for.
